Position-only cube stacking · UR10e + Tesollo DELTO DG-5F

Stack a 34 mm cube on a 34 mm cube, within 5 mm

UR10e arm, Tesollo DELTO DG-5F hand, 26-dimensional action space, OmniReset four-family reset distribution. Every number here is read from the artefact that produced it: TensorBoard event files, eval JSON, per-episode render sidecars.

The reset banks now hold the cube. Every “grasped” reset family was releasing it one control step after the reset — measured with the null action, not inferred — and a constant closing bias on the hand action puts the grip back. The Physics tab has the measurement and the before/after clips.

Six genuine stacks from the final policy, cut from a 51-episode rollout of the last checkpoint. Eleven episodes succeeded; six ran past the 10-step boundary below which a success is only a reset that spawned solved — 21, 22, 32, 35 and 42 steps, final errors 2.6 to 4.9 mm against a 5 mm tolerance. Every one ends with the cube at rest and the hand withdrawn (149–176 mm away, 0.0–2 mm/s): the success predicate requires neither, so release is measured rather than assumed.

Environment QA

Does the reset actually hold the cube?

No policy is involved on this page. Each measurement resets into one family and then applies the null action for a full episode, so whatever the cube does belongs to the environment, not to an actor. Zero action is the right probe precisely because it is not a policy.

Near-Goal and Near-Object look stable only because their cube is already resting on the base cube or on the table. Stable Grasp is the only family whose cube starts in the air, and it slides out of the hand. A 30 g cube weighs 0.29 N; no fingertip in any family carries a tenth of that.

The closing bias that fixes it

The hand runs RelativeJointPositionAction with use_zero_offset=True, so target = measured + scale×action and the first apply_actions() after every reset overwrites the set point the bank restored. At action 0 the PD error is zero and so is the grip force. A constant closing offset restores exactly what the recorder captured — a set point held a fixed angle ahead of the measurement. It is a bias, not a constraint: the policy still commands [−0.1 + bias, +0.1 + bias] per step, so opening stays available.

Palm-to-cube shrinking is the signature of a real grasp: the fingers draw the cube into the grasp centre instead of letting it fall away from a hand that is also drifting.

Is the carry being learned?

Stable-Grasp success is a step function — flat at zero until the policy can both carry the cube and place it inside 5 mm — so it carries no information while the runs are still learning to carry. Progress is measured against each episode’s own starting distance, so a policy that changes nothing scores zero however extreme its endpoints look, and it moves continuously.

What a controller can achieve, with no policy at all

A scripted proportional controller: it knows the goal exactly, has no exploration problem and no reward to satisfy. It answers a question a training curve cannot — whether a family is hard to learn or not reachable as configured, which look identical at 0% and call for opposite responses. Near-Goal is the positive control: it scores, so the environment can produce a success.

Reset familyOSC stiffnessEpisodePath nMedian errorSuccess

The Stable-Grasp rows floor near 60 mm because this controller drives only the three translational action dimensions and leaves rotation at zero, so the cube it carries stays at the ~71° tilt its reset gave it — and a cube that tilted cannot seat flat however its centre is placed. Near-Goal starts nearly level (0.31 rad), which is why the same controller reaches 11 mm there. A policy commands all six dimensions, so this is a limit of the probe, not of the task.

The reward chain

Every arc from "hand anywhere" to "cube stacked" needs a term, or the policy stops where the reward stops paying. Two arcs had none. Each weight is sized against the same measured action_rate penalty of −2.8e-3 per step.

The two rows added here are fingertip_object_distance and dense_success_reward_fine. Without the first, ee_asset_distance reads only the palm body, so parking the open hand on the cube is already optimal and the fingers never close. Without the second, the goal shaping's length scale is 200× the tolerance it is scored against.
