The 9–10% is not a policy achievement at all

Found by inspecting the video, not the numbers

Rendering 43 near-goal episodes produced 3 scored successes. All three ran exactly 10 frames. An episode is 160 steps, and failed episodes in the same run ran 130–160. The Play config ends an episode successfully via consecutive_success_state_with_min_length(num_consecutive_successes=5, min_episode_length=10) — so 10 frames means the success predicate was already satisfied at the earliest instant it could be checked.

These are near-goal resets that spawned already inside the 5 mm tolerance, sat still for the minimum episode length, and were counted as wins. The cube was stacked before the episode began.

That resolves the whole result: trained 9.05% and untrained 10.13% are not two policies performing similarly — they are the same property of the reset distribution, measured twice. The near-goal family carries a free ~10% that no policy has to earn.

Successful episodes: ep19 pos_err 3.48 mm · ep24 2.83 mm · ep30 4.98 mm, each with euler_xy ≈ 0 and each 10 frames long.

prescored_resets_not_stacking.mp4 — the 3 scored episodes, renamed from successes.mp4 once the 10-frame pattern was found. It is not footage of cube stacking and is shipped with a README saying so.

The headline, stated plainly

402 iterations produced no improvement. Trained 9.05% vs untrained 10.13% on near-goal — −1.09 pp, z = −0.75, p = 0.454. The only non-zero cell in the whole table is the family where the cube already starts inside the 5 mm tolerance, and an inert policy scores the same there. Nothing propagated backwards from the goal, which is precisely the mechanism OmniReset claims.

Hand motion rose 11.5×. The policy travelled a long way from its initialisation — it simply did not travel in a direction that raises success. Action magnitudes are large and roughly uniform across all four families (arm 0.48–0.67, hand 0.63–0.82): it acts vigorously from every starting distribution and completes the task from none.

A confirming signature

ever-during exceeds held-at-end only in the trained arm (9.30% vs 9.00%). The untrained policy is identical on both, because it never disturbs anything. The trained one sometimes reaches success and then knocks the cube back out — the expected sign for a policy that actively pushes.

Why this is a result about compute, not about the port

Every stage demonstrably works: banks load, the reward fires, the success predicate is computed identically in training and evaluation, and the policy visibly changes. What is missing is experience — ~26M environment-steps at 2048 parallel environments, the bottom of the paper's swept range, for 402 of 2000 iterations. That is roughly two orders of magnitude below the runs the paper reports, and it is the constraint the 47.4 s/iteration measurement predicted from the start.

The methodological finding, which is the transferable part

One fixed untrained policy measured 5.67% → 7.69% → 10.13% as n grew from 194 to 829. Every intermediate conclusion drawn at n≈195 was wrong — including the one that flattered the result ("+3.60 pp, p = 0.052, suggestive"). The only reason this report is not overclaiming is that the final comparison was re-specified to 800 episodes per arm on power-analysis grounds before the numbers came in, rather than after seeing which way they pointed.

The paper's cube does not fit this hand

Measured directly off the USD meshes with pxr: InsertiveCube and ReceptiveCube are both exactly 40.0 mm on every axis, and neither authors a root MassAPI.

40 mm sits inside the DG-5F's validated grasp window of 24.67–41.48 mm DeltoBlock/metadata.yaml:6-7 and under its 59 mm maximum_aperture, so the paper's own asset looked usable. It is not.

not_far=0, above_ground=0 means the cube left the grasp pose and reached the ground, in every environment of every batch. The same line for deltoblock reads not_far=2 above_ground=2 — two envs hold. The window is a geometric reachability bound, not a holding guarantee, and 40 mm sits 1.5 mm below its top.

Decision

Use the paper's cube asset at scale=0.85 → 34.0 mm, mass 0.03 kg — geometrically and inertially identical to deltoblock, the object this hand's grip-force budget was actually sized against. A stated, forced deviation: the paper's cube is sized for a Robotiq 2F-85 parallel jaw, ours for a Tesollo DG-5F. The task — stack one cube on another — is unchanged.

Context: the grasp path was already the weak link

This is not a regression introduced by the port. The branch documents its own sampler yield on its own reference object delto_cfg.py:600-632:

"10x reproduces across three seeds (2/12, 1/12, 1/12 -- roughly 11 percent per-episode) with the
 thumb gap collapsing 1.267 -> 0.006 rad and holding through gravity AND shake, 7 bodies in
 contact, no limit cycle. 20x is WORSE (0/180, fewer bodies, lower peak force) ...
 11 percent is well below the 60 percent acceptance bar"

Separately, DeltoHand/metadata.yaml:64 stamps the grasp centre "FK-DERIVED, NOT YET EMPIRICALLY CONFIRMED" while the same file's header claims replay "holds under gravity". The two statements are inconsistent; the measurements above side with the cautious one.

Is a 5%-acceptance bank actually usable?

Low yield is a wall-clock problem, not automatically a quality one — every recorded grasp passed the shake test. But it could mean the only candidates surviving are one face at one angle, and the paper's §4.3 ablation shows a narrowed grasp distribution costs both sample efficiency and final success rate. So the bank was measured, not assumed.

The limitation, stated rather than buried

Coverage is structurally narrower than the paper's "Broad" setting, and it is the hand, not a tuning miss. DeltoHand/metadata.yaml zeroes all three top-down sweep knobs, with the reason given in the file: "Generic top-down position/height/tilt and standoff sweeps move this non-parallel hand off its narrow opposing-pad basin; keep them disabled for DELTO." The DG-5F trades grasp diversity for grasp reliability. Approach from below (−z) is geometrically impossible for a top-down sampler on a resting object in any case.

By the paper's own ablation this predicts somewhat worse sample efficiency than their 2F-85 cube run. That is a property of the port's hardware, and it belongs in the results up front rather than being discovered later in a learning curve.

Untested lever, if sample efficiency disappoints

The four grasp_topdown_* knobs are exposed as Hydra overrides precisely so they can be swept per object. The warnings that led to zeroing them were written about a 200 mm table leg; a cube is a far more forgiving shape, so a small non-zero roll/pitch sweep might buy diversity without leaving the opposing-pad basin. That is a measurable experiment, not a guess — and it has not been run.

C1 only has to place a cube on a table and put the arm somewhere legal. The other three all require a sustained grasp, and that is a 3-order-of-magnitude difference in cost.

This is inherent, not a defect in the port

The branch's own measured acceptance on the leg pair is 0.83% (40/4799) for the near-object family against 57.21% for reaching — a 69× spread between the grasped and ungrasped families. Our C3 sits in the same regime, for two compounding reasons: the hand holds this cube about 5% of the time at best (that is the ceiling), and C3 is strictly harder than the sampler that produced that 5% — the sampler uses the hand-only articulation with a fixed root, while C3 must also drive the full UR10e to a mid-air grasp pose and keep the grasp through the arm's own dynamics.

What was done about it

Acceptance-bound work scales linearly in parallel environments, and the dev card was already at 14.8 of 16.4 GB at 1024 envs — no headroom to buy. So an RTX A6000 (46 GB) was rented at $0.4556/h and the three grasped banks moved there at 4096 envs.

Bank sizes are 2000 states for the grasped families against 4000 for C1 — a deliberate, stated reduction. The paper never states bank sizes; the deployment guide suggests 10,000. At training time the four banks are sampled uniformly at 0.25 each across roughly 1.6M episode starts, so every state is reused hundreds of times whether the bank holds 2000 or 10,000.

Two harness bugs this stage exposed

  • The verifier passed on a stale artefact. After a fast-failing rerun the previous run's file was still on disk at full size, so C1 "passed" in 7 seconds having recorded nothing. Every stage now deletes its output first and requires the artefact to be newer than the stage that produced it.
  • A killed Isaac process wedges the GPU. kill -9 left 13.8 GB held; the next run came up with _physics_sim_view = None and died at create_rigid_body_view — which reads like a config error and is not. There is now a VRAM preflight that refuses to start below 8 GB free and names the offending PIDs.

A hypothesis that did not survive its own test

Grasp sampling accepts at 5.0%. The three grasp-dependent reset families accept at roughly 1% — same object, same grasps, same hand. A 5× gap between sampling a grasp and replaying it needs an explanation.

The mechanism found

DeltoGraspSamplingCfg rebinds two joints before it samples — rj_dg_1_4 and rj_dg_5_4, the distal joints of the thumb-side pair, from ~0.17 to ~1.70 stiffness — and the branch records why the stock values do not work:

"realised torque is bounded by stiffness * 0.1. At 0.1698 that is 0.0170 N.m -- matching the
 measured applied torque to four decimals -- against a 0.01 N.m friction floor ...
 The joint sat frozen at ~1.05 rad, velocity exactly 0.000, in EVERY
 velocity/deadband/stiffness combination swept."

That rebind is scoped to the sampler alone. Every reset-state and RL config uses the stock actuator. So a grasp is sampled and shake-validated by a hand that can close its thumb, then replayed — during reset recording and during RL — by a hand that cannot.

The test, and the result

512 envs, 420 s per arm, same object, same grasp bank, only the two stiffnesses differing. C3 accepts a state only if the grasp holds, so its acceptance rate is the hold rate — there is no interpretation layer between the number and the claim.

ArmStates accepted in 420 sPoisson noise
stock actuator23±4.8
grasp-matched actuator31±5.6
Verdict

+35%, but the difference is 8 ± 7.4 — about 1.1σ. Not significant. A run this size cannot separate a 35% effect from chance; it would need roughly an order of magnitude more states per arm. The hypothesis is not supported. The thumb-side stiffness is not what accounts for the sampling-to-reset gap. The likelier explanation remains the one already on the table: the sampler uses the hand-only articulation with a fixed root, while the reset families must additionally drive the full UR10e to the grasp pose and hold it through the arm's own dynamics.

Adopted anyway — for a different reason

  • The grasp bank was recorded with this actuator. Replaying those postures under a different one is a real mismatch whatever it costs in acceptance rate.
  • It cannot exceed the force budget. At 10× the commanded torque already exceeds the 0.06 N·m effort_limit_sim, so delivered torque is still capped there and the ~2.2 N fingertip ceiling is unchanged. It lets the joint reach a budget it already has.
  • A frozen thumb handicaps the policy, not just the recorder — independently of how many reset states get banked.

Not claimed: that this speeds up bank generation, or improves final success rate. Neither is established.

A side-finding worth more than the hypothesis

Fewer environments run faster here. The rented A6000 at 2048 envs managed ~1.2 states/min; the dev card at 512 managed 3.3–4.4. Acceptance is per-env-per-episode, but per-step cost grows faster than that, so extra parallelism was actively hurting. Both boxes now run 512 — which also means the paper's "scale the number of parallel environments" advice, which is about PPO batch size, does not transfer to reset-state recording.

The training curve is misleading, and here is why

Mean reward falls from −0.44 to −3.28 over 200 iterations. Taken alone that reads as divergence. The decomposition says otherwise:

The task terms are flat. The entire decline is the action_rate penalty tracking gSDE's exploration noise as it inflates from 0.75 to 2.65 — the entropy bonus dominating a weak advantage signal, which is what a batch two orders of magnitude below the paper's produces.

Why that curve cannot answer "did it learn"

Evaluation is deterministic. rsl_rl's get_inference_policy returns act_inference, which is the actor's mean action — the exploration noise that dominates the training reward never enters it. Training return and evaluated behaviour are measuring different things, so the only honest way to separate "learned a little" from "learned nothing" is to evaluate an untrained checkpoint against a trained one under identical deterministic conditions. That comparison is what this run reports, rather than an inference from the curve.

The baseline is the point

An untrained policy scores 5.67% from near-goal resets: those states begin close enough that a policy doing nothing useful still leaves the cube seated some of the time. Reported alone, 9.23% would have looked like a modest success. It is only meaningful against that floor — which is why the baseline was run at all, and why a +63% relative improvement is still not a claim.

Resolving an effect this size needs roughly 800 episodes per arm (z ≈ 2.7), so the final comparison was re-specified from 256 to 800. Evaluation costs a fraction of training; under-powering the one measurement the result rests on would be a poor trade.

What this says

Training does something, and it stops early. This is the shape the paper's own §4.3 ablation predicts for an under-resourced run: small environment counts "can make progress at solving the task from Near-Goal states" but fail to scale to the full multi-stage task. This run sits at 2048 envs — the bottom of their swept range — for 300 iterations rather than 2000.

The degenerate-policy alternative: ruled out by measurement

Beating the baseline at near-goal is not by itself competence — some resets spawn the cube already inside the 5 mm threshold, so a policy that learned to hold still would collect those for free. Measured directly:

A correction that goes against the result

Re-running the same untrained checkpoint gave 7.69% where the first measurement gave 5.67% — identical settings, only the seed differed. That 2.02 pp spread is the noise floor at n≈195, comparable to the entire effect being measured.

My earlier comparison had used the low draw as its baseline. Pooling both untrained runs gives 6.68% (26/389) against a trained 10.29% (61/593): +3.60 pp, z = 1.94, p = 0.052. The effect is smaller than the +4.62 pp reported earlier and still not significant. Not an arithmetic error — an under-powered baseline, which is precisely the failure a single measurement invites.

What the combination supports

The policy acts (9.4× hand motion) and scores modestly higher, but does not place accurately — position error median got worse and the full task stays at 0%. It has learned to engage the cube, not to place it. That is a more specific claim than "it learned a bit": it says which half was learned and which was not.

A detail a single number would have hidden

Position error median got worse (13.5 → 18.4 mm) while the success rate went up. Those reconcile only if the distribution became bimodal — more episodes landing inside the 5 mm threshold and more ending far away, with fewer in between. That is what an emerging but unreliable manipulation policy looks like: sometimes decisive, sometimes shoving the cube off. A median alone reads "worse"; a success rate alone reads "better". Both are recorded, with p90, for exactly this reason.

ever_during equals held_at_end in both arms, so nothing is being knocked off after a momentary success — the failure mode this branch's sticky-OR grading would have concealed is not occurring here.

The compute gap, stated plainly

The paper's ablation sweeps 2048–65 536 parallel environments and finds that small counts still solve near-goal states but fail the full task from reaching states. This run is at 2048 — the bottom of that range — for a few hundred iterations rather than 2000.

So the honest target here is near-goal competence, which is exactly what the paper's Fig. 8 shows emerging first, and success is reported decomposed by reset family for the same reason the paper decomposes it. A single full-task number would hide whichever way the result went.

Two variants, one shared pipeline

Success is computed in one place, ProgressContext.__call__ mdp/rewards.py:

e_x, e_y, _ = euler_xyz_from_quat(...)
euler_xy_distance   = |wrap_to_pi(e_x)| + |wrap_to_pi(e_y)|   # yaw is dropped, deliberately
orientation_aligned = euler_xy_distance < success_orientation_threshold
position_aligned    = xyz_distance      < success_position_threshold
success             = orientation_aligned & position_aligned

Yaw is already discarded upstream, so the oriented variant constrains roll and pitch only — "a chosen face must end up facing up, but the cube may spin freely about the vertical". That is exactly what the paper's "with a desired orientation" (§4.1) means as this codebase implements it, and it is what a red-face-up criterion would be.

 Position-only (primary)Oriented (paper-faithful)
Success testposition onlyposition and roll/pitch
success_orientation_threshold7.0 rad0.05 rad

Why 7.0 and not infinity

euler_xy_distance is a sum of two wrap_to_pi magnitudes and is therefore bounded by 2π ≈ 6.2832. 7.0 is unconditionally satisfied, exactly like infinity, while staying a finite number that logging and JSON serialisation handle without special cases.

Dropping orientation is a deletion, not a re-weighting

The paper's Eq. 5 is r_dist = λ·½·[exp(−|x_err|/σ) + exp(−|θ_err|/σ)]. The orientation-free variant drops the second summand and the ½, so the position term keeps full scale. Setting std_angle huge instead is not equivalent — it leaves a constant +0.5 and halves the position gradient.

Supporting evidence from the branch

On the table-leg task, adding an orientation requirement cost a 4.5× precision penalty — 26.4 mm median against 4.8 mm without. That is the best in-repo reason to expect the position-only variant to be markedly the easier of the two.

D9 Cap scripts used pkill (SIGTERM); Isaac ignores it. The C3 cap logged success while the recorder kept running, so the next family never got the GPU. Found by verifying rather than trusting the log. FIXED
D8 — and a hypothesis rejected before acting on it

The same range looked like it might also explain the 5% → ~1% collapse in reset-bank acceptance: if 83% of environments carry an unholdable cube, then 0.17 × 5% ≈ 0.85% reproduces the observed rate almost exactly. A very tempting fit.

It is wrong. ResetStatesBaseEventCfg contains no mass randomization at all — recording runs at the fixed 0.030 kg spawn mass, exactly like the grasp sampler. The arithmetic agreeing was coincidence. This was checked before restarting any recording on the strength of it, which would have cost about an hour.

D6 — the mechanism, because it will bite anyone extending this

IsaacLab defers instantiation of non-prestartup class event terms to a callback on the Omniverse timeline PLAY event. MultiResetManager is such a term, so it is constructed inside that callback — and this branch already documents that the timeline dispatcher prints and continues rather than propagating. The FileNotFoundError is therefore swallowed, term_cfg.func is left as the class rather than an instance, and the next EventManager.apply(mode="reset") calls it as a plain function — landing in __init__, which complains about the first keyword it sees.

Confirmed rather than inferred: a diagnostic that inspects the manager shows the term correctly instantiated and reset() succeeding when the banks exist, and reproduces the TypeError when they do not. When you see it, check that <dataset_dir>/Resets/<pair>/resets_<Type>.pt exists — nothing in the error names the file, the pair, or the directory.

On D1

The correction changes the reach reward's reference point for every task on this robot, including the table-leg ones. That is deliberate — the point it referenced does not exist — and it is one line to revert. It cannot invalidate any grasp dataset: the sampler reads grasp_center_offset from Robots/DeltoHand/metadata.yaml, never this field.

What OmniReset actually does

The method's claim is that long-horizon exploration collapses if you reset the simulator into the diverse states that make up the task, rather than shaping rewards or supplying demonstrations. Rewards are task-agnostic and shared across every task in the paper without modification.

Four reset families, sampled as a flat mixture

BankFamilyHow it is generatedNeeds
C1Reachingobject on the table, gripper anywhere in the workspace—
C2Near-objectgripper at a grasp point + jitter, open or closed at randomC1 + grasps
C3Stable graspobject in mid-air, gripper at a feasible grasp pointgrasps
C4Near-goalobject at a dislodged-from-goal offset, gripper in contactpartial assemblies + grasps

Training samples Uniform(C1 ∪ C2 ∪ C3 ∪ C4) at 0.25 each — no curriculum, no ordering, no graph between states. The backwards curriculum the paper describes (solve near-goal first, then the whole task) is emergent, not imposed. Evaluation uses C1 alone, so the headline number is measured from the full-task reaching distribution.

Reward, verbatim from the paper

r = r_success + r_dist + r_reach + r_smooth + r_term

r_reach   = 0.1 · (1 − tanh(‖p_ee − p_obj‖ / σ))
r_dist    = 0.1 · ½ · [exp(−‖x_err‖/σ) + exp(−‖θ_err‖/σ)]
r_success = 1.0 · 1[position and orientation thresholds satisfied]
r_smooth  = −1e-4‖a‖² − 1e-3‖a − a_prev‖² − 1e-2‖q̇‖²
r_term    = −100 · 1[abnormal state]

These map one-to-one onto ee_asset_distance_tanh, dense_success_reward, success_reward and the clamped penalty terms in rl_state_cfg.py:561-603.

What the paper does not specify

No PPO hyperparameters, no network sizes, no episode length, no σ, no success thresholds, no cube dimensions, and no compute figure for cube stacking. §4.1 defers to the code release. The repository, not the paper, is the source of truth for every task parameter here.

Deliberately skipped

The sim-to-real dynamics curriculum. The paper states its own simulation-only results are trained without it (§5, §A.3.9), and this is a simulation deliverable — the curriculum would only spend compute.

Reproducing this

Branch cube-stack/delto, a worktree off origin/dexreset/v2-resets. Every command takes env.scene.insertive_object=cube34 env.scene.receptive_object=cube34; grasp sampling takes env.scene.object=cube34.

1 — Grasp bank

python scripts_v2/tools/record_grasps.py \
  --task OmniReset-Delto-GraspSampling-v0 \
  --num_envs 1024 --num_grasps 1000 --headless \
  --dataset_dir ./Datasets_ur10e_delto/OmniReset \
  env.scene.object=cube34

2 — Near-goal offsets, then the four reset banks

python scripts_v2/tools/record_partial_assemblies.py \
  --task OmniReset-CubeStack-PartialAssemblies-v0 ...

python scripts_v2/tools/record_reset_states.py \
  --task OmniReset-UR10eDelto-CubeStack-<Family>-v0 \
  --num_envs 1024 --num_reset_states 4000 --headless ...

Order matters: C1 before C2, and the grasp bank before C2, C3 and C4.

3 — Train

python scripts/reinforcement_learning/rsl_rl/train.py \
  --task OmniReset-UR10eDelto-CubeStackNoOrient-RelCartesianOSC-State-v0 \
  --num_envs 2048 --max_iterations 2000 --seed 42 --headless \
  env.scene.insertive_object=cube34 env.scene.receptive_object=cube34

Swap the task id for ...-CubeStack-RelCartesianOSC-State-v0 to train the oriented variant. Pass --max_iterations explicitly — the config's ceiling is 40 000 and the run will never stop otherwise.

You will hit this first

Training aborts immediately with ValueError: Unknown standard deviation type: gsde. The agent config uses gSDE — the paper's §3.3 exploration decision — and upstream rsl-rl-lib on PyPI does not implement it. uwlab_rl/setup.py pins the right source:

pip install --no-deps --force-reinstall \
  "rsl-rl-lib @ git+https://github.com/UW-Lab/rsl_rl.git@main"

--no-deps is required — installing it with dependencies drags torch to 2.13.0+cu130, which Isaac Sim 5.1 does not expect. Re-pin torch 2.7.0+cu128 afterwards.

4 — Evaluate, decomposed and against a baseline

python eval_policy.py --task ...-State-Play-v0 --checkpoint model_400.pt \
  --num_envs 128 --episodes 800 --reset_type ObjectPartiallyAssembledEEGrasped

Two things this run learned the hard way. Always evaluate the untrained model_0 too: near-goal resets score ~10% with an inert policy, so a trained number means nothing without that floor. And use ≥ 800 episodes: at n≈195 the noise floor is ~2 pp, comparable to any effect worth detecting.

Two traps worth carrying over

The collision stack. _apply_delto_collision_stack_size pins 3.75 GiB, the maximum legal value, sized for 24 GB cards; on a 16 GB card PhysX OOMs mid-build. Reducing it is safe only for small probes — an undersized stack does not crash, it logs "Contacts have been dropped" and silently continues, so every bank here is checked for that string before being accepted.

Detached processes. vast.ai reaps setsid/nohup/ tmux children when the spawning ssh closes, so long runs there must go through --onstart under the container's init.
